One card for the money a family already shares.

A family does not spend like one person. Kinnect is one line, with a role for each person and a goal they can all see. The (i) explains that goal in plain words.

The story, in one household

Entertainment is $4,000 a year. A trip is a slice of that. If the Alvarez family puts a $2,867 vacation on the card and takes 24 months to pay it at 22.25%, the interest is about $710. Saving $240 a month pays for the trip before they leave. That is the short-term goal on the phone. The (i) says this in the same words to a parent and to Maya.

The $77,280 and the year chart are the case artifacts. The $710 is our math on a sample trip, using the Federal Reserve’s May 2025 rate for cards that are being charged interest. It is an illustration, not a measured pilot.

How we read the artifacts, and the outside numbers
  1. Case Data Artifact 4, U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Family spending by year, in thousands: 2012 and 2013 $51, 2014 $53, 2015 $56, 2016 $57, 2017 $60, 2018 $61, 2019 $63, 2020 $61, 2021 $67, 2022 $73, 2023 $77. Rise from $51,000 to $77,000 is $26,000, or 51%.
  2. Case Data Artifact 5, U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2023. Average annual spend $77,280. Housing $25,000, other $15,000, transportation $13,000, food $10,000, insurance $10,000, entertainment $4,000. Those six figures sum to $77,000. Housing + transportation + food = $48,000, which is 62% of $77,280. Entertainment is 5%.
  3. Case Data Artifact 1. The five scenes above are the ones printed in the packet. Kinnect answers them with roles on one line, not with a separate product for each scene.
  4. Outside the packet, only where a goal needs a concrete example. A $2,867 vacation is Allianz via Investopedia, 2025. Interest uses payment = P · r(1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1), P = 2,867, r = 0.2225/12, n = 24. Payment is about $149. Interest is about $711. The 22.25% rate is Federal Reserve G.19, May 2025, accounts assessed interest.
